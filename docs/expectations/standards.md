# Standards & Culture

## Before you arrive

- Get enough sleep. Live production requires focus.
- Review the run of show if one is available.
- Arrive at call time, not service time.

## Wear your Livestream badge

When you are serving, your **Livestream badge stays on at all times**. The badge is not just an ID, it tells everyone on campus who you are and that you are part of the team running the service.

While you are wearing that badge, **you represent the church**. How you talk, how you carry yourself, how you treat people, all of it matters. People will form an impression of The Garden Church based on you.

## Be warm, welcoming, and inviting

This team is not just behind the gear. We are part of the front door of the church.

- Greet people you do not recognize. Smile, say hi, ask their name.
- Be aggressively welcoming to new faces. Make sure they feel seen and known.
- Invite people in. If someone seems curious about what we do, tell them. If they would be a good fit for the team, tell them how to get involved.
- Treat every interaction as if it is the reason that person came back next week.

## Care for each other

We're a team, and that means caring about each other off the headset too. **Use the team GroupMe to stay connected, not just coordinated.**

## Dress code

Wardrobe should be **dark or black, and modest**.

- Dark / black tops and bottoms keep you visually neutral on camera and around stage.
- **Pants or jeans are preferred over shorts.** Save shorts for the beach.
- Modest means modest. If you are unsure, ask before you serve, not after.

## Know the service plan

Before you walk in, **be familiar with the service plan.** Planning Center Services has the full plan, including the song list and order, available in the Services app.

You don't need to know every transition by heart, but you should know:

- The songs being played and the rough order
- Anything noted about flow, special moments, or video focus
- Any changes from a typical week

The service plan will be reviewed at the team huddle, but **the huddle is the time to ask final questions, not to read the plan for the first time.** Come prepared so the huddle is for clarification, not catch-up.

### Worship songs

We are not recording or producing the worship sets yet, **but we will be soon.** Start building the habit now: when you check the service plan, glance at the songs and get familiar with them at a basic level. Knowing the dynamics of a song (where it builds, where it lands) will eventually shape the camera work and the stream.

## Team huddle

The full production team huddles at **7:30 AM behind the stage**. This is where the day's plan, run of show, and any last-minute changes get communicated.

Be there on time. Showing up to huddle late means the team has to repeat itself, and you walk into service already behind. The huddle is also your last clear window to ask questions about the day's flow, video focus, or anything else before service starts.

## Phones away

**Keep your phone put away during service** unless using it is part of your role (running a control app, checking a stream, etc.). Scrolling at your station looks bad on camera, looks bad to guests walking by, and pulls your focus off the service.

If you do need your phone for the role, that's fine. Otherwise, pocket it.

## Discretion

!!! warning "What happens in the booth stays in the booth"
    Production teams hear and see things others don't. Hot mics before service, conversations near the stage, off-stage moments, things shared in confidence with a pastor — none of that is content for outside the team. **Use discretion.**

## Your station

You and your station should be **presentable, organized, and cable-managed** at all times.

- **Tidy as you go.** Loose cables, stacked coffee cups, and gear sprawl all read as "this team doesn't care." We do.
- **Cable management matters.** Run cables cleanly, tape down anything that's a trip hazard, and don't leave coiled cables piled in walkways.
- **Drinks and snacks are fine, but keep them tucked away.** Out of frame, off the gear, and off the table surface where you're working. A water bottle on the floor next to your chair is great. A water bottle on top of the switcher is not.
- **Your station is your responsibility.** If something is broken, dirty, or missing, flag it and fix it.

## Sightlines

You're working in a room full of people who came here for the service, not for us. **Be considerate of guests' sightlines** at all times.

- Don't stand or move where you'll block people's view of the stage.
- If you have to cross the room, take the path that gets you out of sight fastest.
- Crouch when moving in front of seated guests during service.
- Cameras, stands, and gear should be placed to minimize visual obstruction.

## On camera

Not everything that happens in the room belongs on the stream. **Use judgment about what we put on camera.**

!!! note "Default to discretion"
    - During pastoral moments (altar calls, prayer, breakdowns), default to wide and respectful, not close and intrusive.
    - Children should not be the focus of a shot without context.
    - If something private is unfolding (a hard conversation, someone visibly upset), pull off it.
    - When in doubt, ask Jay or hold a wider frame until you can.

## During the service

- Communicate problems immediately, don't sit on an issue.
- If you don't know, ask. Guessing on live production is how things go wrong.

## After the service

- Don't leave until teardown is complete.
- Flag any gear issues to the Team Lead before you go.
- Debrief honestly, what went well, what didn't.

## General

We serve the church with excellence. That means caring about the details, being on time, and treating the gear and the space with respect.
