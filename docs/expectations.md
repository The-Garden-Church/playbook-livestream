# Expectations

Serving on the Livestream Team is a real commitment. This page outlines what we expect from every Team Member and what you can expect from the team in return.

## Communication: GroupMe

All team communication happens in the team **GroupMe** channel. It is the primary way the team coordinates, and staying in the loop is part of the commitment.

**Install the app and turn on notifications.** Email and SMS forwarding don't cover it. You need to be in the app.

- [GroupMe for iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/groupme/id392796698)
- [GroupMe for Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.groupme.android)

You are expected to monitor the channel and respond in a timely manner, especially in the day or two leading up to a service you are scheduled for.

**Acknowledge posts with a reaction.** When a message goes out, drop a thumbs up or another quick reaction so the sender knows it was seen. This matters most for team-wide posts or messages directed at you. Without a reaction, the sender has to follow up to confirm you saw it. A reaction takes a second and keeps the team moving.

### Care for each other

We care about each other off the headset too. **Use the team GroupMe to stay connected, not only coordinated.**

## Scheduling: Planning Center

The team is scheduled through **Planning Center Services**. Install the app, sign in, and turn on notifications.

- [Planning Center Services for iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/planning-center-services/id509143998)
- [Planning Center Services for Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ministrycentered.PlanningCenter)

### Responding to serve requests

When a serve request comes through, **respond in a reasonable timeframe.** Do not let it sit for weeks.

- Accept or decline. Both are valid responses.
- If you need to decline, **do it with enough lead time** for the Team Lead to find someone else.
- The team can plan around a "no" given two weeks out. The team cannot plan around a "no" given Saturday night.

### Keep your unavailability up to date

In Planning Center, **block out the dates you know you cannot serve** (vacations, work travel, family commitments, etc.) and **keep that list current.** This is the easiest way to avoid being scheduled for a date you cannot make, and it saves the Team Lead from chasing replacements.

### If you become unavailable after being scheduled

Once you are on the schedule, you are committed to that date. If something legitimately changes and you can no longer serve, **it is your responsibility to find a replacement.**

1. Post in the team GroupMe chat and ask if anyone can fill in for you.
2. Once someone agrees, confirm the swap with the Team Lead so the schedule is updated in Planning Center.
3. If you cannot find anyone, tell the Team Lead as early as possible so they can help cover.

### If you're not scheduled, you're off

When you are not on the schedule, you are off. **You are not expected to pitch in** if you happen to be at church.

Be a normal church-goer that weekend. Sit with your family, drop into your community group, be present in service. The team would rather schedule deeply enough that we don't need pitch-ins than have anyone serving out of guilt.

If we are routinely coming up short and pitching in feels necessary, that's a recruiting problem, not a Sunday problem. Flag it to the Team Lead.

## Attendance and arrival

Reliability is one of the most important traits on a live production team. Last-minute no-shows create real problems. We understand life happens, just communicate early.

**Arrival time is 6:00 AM.** We have a lot to set up before doors open and the team is counting on you to be there.

If something comes up and you are going to be late, **post in the team GroupMe channel** so the rest of the team knows and can adjust. A heads-up gives the team time to cover.

**Regular tardiness will not be accepted.** Live production runs on trust. The team has to know they can count on you to show up when you said you would. Repeated lateness will be addressed by the Team Lead and may result in being removed from the schedule.

## Know the service plan

Before you walk in, **be familiar with the service plan.** Planning Center Services has the full plan, including the song list and order, available in the Services app.

You don't need to know every transition by heart, but you should know:

- The songs being played and the rough order
- Anything noted about flow, special moments, or video focus
- Any changes from a typical week

The service plan will be reviewed at the team huddle, but **the huddle is the time to ask final questions, not to read the plan for the first time.** Come prepared so the huddle is for clarification, not catch-up.

### Worship songs

We are not recording or producing the worship sets yet, **but we will be soon.** Start the habit now. When you check the service plan, glance at the songs and get familiar with them. Knowing the dynamics of a song, including where it builds and where it lands, will shape the camera work and the stream.

## Day-of standards

### Wear your Livestream badge

When you are serving, your **Livestream badge stays on at all times.** The badge identifies you as part of the team running the service.

While you are wearing that badge, **you represent the church.** How you talk, how you carry yourself, and how you treat people all matter. People will form an impression of The Garden Church based on you.

### Be warm, welcoming, and inviting

This team works behind the gear and at the front door of the church.

- Greet people you do not recognize. Smile, say hi, ask their name.
- Welcome new faces actively. Make sure they feel seen and known.
- Invite people in. If someone seems curious about what we do, tell them. If they would be a good fit for the team, tell them how to get involved.
- Treat every interaction like it's the reason that person came back the next week.

### Dress code

Wardrobe should be **dark or black.**

- Dark or black tops and bottoms keep you visually neutral on camera and around stage.
- **Pants or jeans are preferred over shorts.**

### Phones away

**Keep your phone put away during service** unless using it is part of your role (running a control app, checking a stream, etc.). Scrolling at your station looks bad on camera, looks bad to guests walking by, and pulls your focus off the service.

If you do need your phone for the role, that's fine. Otherwise, pocket it.

### Your station

You and your station should be **presentable, organized, and cable-managed** at all times.

- **Tidy as you go.** Loose cables, stacked coffee cups, and gear sprawl signal a team that doesn't care. We do.
- **Cable management matters.** Run cables cleanly, tape down anything that's a trip hazard, and don't leave coiled cables piled in walkways.
- **Drinks and snacks stay tucked away.** Out of frame, off the gear, and off the working surface. Keep drinks on the floor, not on top of the switcher.
- **Your station is your responsibility.** If something is broken, dirty, or missing, flag it and fix it.

### Sightlines

You're working in a room full of people who came here for the service, not for us. **Be considerate of guests' sightlines** at all times.

- Don't stand or move where you'll block people's view of the stage.
- If you have to cross the room, take the path that gets you out of sight fastest.
- Crouch when moving in front of seated guests during service.
- Cameras, stands, and gear should be placed to minimize visual obstruction.

## Team huddle

The full production team huddles at **7:30 AM behind the stage**. This is where the day's plan, run of show, and any last-minute changes get communicated.

Be there on time. Showing up to huddle late means the team has to repeat itself, and you walk into service already behind. The huddle is also your last clear window to ask questions about the day's flow, video focus, or anything else before service starts.

## On-camera discretion

Not everything that happens in the room belongs on the stream. **Use judgment about what we put on camera.**

!!! note "Default to discretion"
    - During pastoral moments (altar calls, prayer, breakdowns), default to wide and respectful, not close and intrusive.
    - Children should not be the focus of a shot without context.
    - If something private is unfolding (a hard conversation, someone visibly upset), pull off it.
    - When in doubt, ask the Team Lead or hold a wider frame until you can.

!!! warning "What happens in the booth stays in the booth"
    Production teams hear and see things others don't: hot mics before service, conversations near the stage, off-stage moments, things shared in confidence with a pastor. None of that leaves the team. **Use discretion.**

## How we handle problems

If something is wrong, broken, or could be better, **bring it forward.** The Team Lead would rather hear about a problem early and directly than pick it up later from someone else's frustration. Honest feedback makes the team better.

There is a difference between critique and complaining.

- **Critique** is naming a problem and wanting it fixed. Take it to the Team Lead. We will work on it together.
- **Complaining** is talking about a problem with no desire to find a solution. That is what we don't do here.

The back-hallway version of a problem doesn't fix anything and pulls the team's posture down with it. If something is bothering you, bring it to someone who can do something about it.

## Best idea wins

Anyone on this team can have the best idea. It doesn't matter how long you've been around or what your role is. **The best idea wins.**

If you see a better way to do something, say so. Bring it to the Team Lead, raise it in a huddle, drop it in GroupMe. You'll be heard out, and if it's the right call, that's the way we'll do it.

The flip side: not every idea will win. That's fine. Stay open to other people's ideas the same way you want yours to be heard.

## Recruiting is everyone's job

This team will keep growing, and every Team Member is part of that. **You should be thinking about who you could invite to join.**

- The friend who's tech-curious and looking for a way to plug in.
- The person sitting next to you in service who keeps asking what we do.
- The new face in the lobby who you ended up talking to for ten minutes.

Recruiting doesn't mean closing the deal. It means tapping shoulders, starting conversations, and pointing people toward the team. The Team Lead will take it from there.

If we are short on a Sunday, the answer is not to work harder or pitch in to cover. **The answer is to grow the team.** That starts with you.

## Stepping off the team

Seasons change. School, work, family, health, or a needed break are all real reasons to step off the team for a while or for good. **There is no shame in that.** Tell the Team Lead so the team isn't left guessing.

If you need to pause or step off:

1. Talk to the Team Lead directly. A quick GroupMe DM is enough to start the conversation.
2. Give as much notice as you can so the schedule can be adjusted.
3. Finish out anything you're already scheduled for, or help find a replacement.

!!! quote "The door stays open"
    If life shifts again and you want to come back, you're welcome.
