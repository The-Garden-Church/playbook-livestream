## Camera Operator

Responsible for operating an assigned camera and following the shot list and director cues.

### How we use the camera buttons

The [Hardware page](../hardware.md#function-buttons) lists what each button does. Use them on a serve as follows:

- **Function 1 (focus assist).** Use during pre-service to dial in focus on each camera. Anything sharp shows up in green on the camera's screen, so you can confirm focus quickly. **Toggle it off before going live** so you're not distracted by the highlights during service. (The green doesn't appear in the program feed, but turning it off is a good habit.)
- **Function 3 (framing guides).** Use the **top guide line for headspace**. Line up the top of the speaker's head with the top guide. That gives a consistent, well-balanced frame across both cameras.

### Framing and headspace

If you're operating a camera, framing matters as much as focus. The two most important concepts are the **rule of thirds** and **headroom** (the space between the top of the subject's head and the top of the frame).

Before your first shift on camera, work through these:

- [Mastering Headroom: Applying the Rule of Thirds (Connection Card Pro)](https://www.connectioncardpro.com/help/Streaming-Cameras-and-Composition/Mastering-Headroom-Applying-the-Rule-of-Thirds/). Written specifically for church livestreaming. Covers waist-up, knees-up, and elbows-up shots common in worship settings, with a five-point checklist you can run through before going live. Best starting point for new camera operators on the team.
- [A beginner's guide to the rule of thirds (Videomaker)](https://www.videomaker.com/article/c10/14215-a-beginners-guide-to-the-rule-of-thirds/). A broader video composition primer if you want to build the foundation beyond church-specific framing.
- [Headroom (photographic framing) on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headroom_(photographic_framing)). Quick reference for the concept itself.
