# Glossary

A quick reference for the production terms you'll hear around the team. You don't need to memorize this. If you hear a term that isn't here, ask, and we'll add it.

## General production

**Call time.** The time you're expected to be on site and ready to start setting up. For the Livestream Team, call time is 6:00 AM.

**Run of show.** The minute-by-minute plan for a service. What happens, when, in what order.

**Cue.** A signal to do something at a specific moment (cue the camera, cue the slide, cue the bumper).

**Bumper.** A short pre-recorded video or graphic that plays between segments of the service.

**Lower third.** A graphic that appears in the lower portion of the screen, usually showing a name, title, or scripture reference.

**Run of show / cue sheet.** The document or app that tracks every cue for the service.

---

## Audio

**FOH.** Front of House. The main audio mix the room hears through the speakers.

**Stream mix.** The audio mix sent to the livestream, often different from FOH.

**Levels.** How loud or quiet a signal is. "Watch your levels" means make sure nothing is clipping or too quiet.

**Clipping.** Audio that is too loud and distorts. Avoid it.

---

## Video

**Switcher.** The hardware or software that picks which camera or source goes out to the stream. Also called a "vision mixer."

**IMAG.** Image Magnification. The big-screen video of the speaker projected for the room to see.

**Multicam.** Using multiple cameras to capture an event.

**Tally.** The light on a camera that shows it's currently live on the stream.

**Lower third.** See above (it's a video term too). Ours sit at the bottom center of the frame, so keep the speaker's chin above them.

### Shot sizes

Named by where the bottom of the frame cuts across the person. See [Framing the shot](camera-basics/framing.md) for pictures of each.

**EWS (extreme wide shot).** The whole stage or room. The person is small.

**WS (wide shot), also full shot.** Head to toe, with a little floor below the feet.

**MLS (medium long shot), also knee shot.** Knees up. Some people call it an American shot.

**Cowboy shot.** Mid-thigh up. People sometimes use the name loosely for a waist-up shot.

**MS (medium shot).** Waist up.

**MCU (medium close-up).** Chest up.

**CU (close-up).** Head and shoulders.

**ECU (extreme close-up).** Eyes and mouth.

### Composition

**Headroom.** The space between the top of the head and the top of the frame.

**Eye line.** Where the eyes sit in the frame. In a medium shot and tighter, put them near the top third line.

**Rule of thirds.** Splitting the frame into thirds across and down, and placing important things on those lines.

**Lead room, also look room or nose room.** Empty space in front of a person, on the side they're looking or moving toward.

**Cutting at a joint.** When the edge of the frame lands on the neck, elbows, waist, knees or ankles. Avoid it.

**Safe areas (action safe and title safe).** Boxes a little inside the edge of the frame. Keep important action inside action safe, and text inside title safe, so no screen crops them off.

**Camera left / camera right.** Left and right as seen through the camera.

**Stage left / stage right.** Left and right as seen by the person on stage, looking out at the room. The opposite of camera left and right.

### Common director calls

Many teams use these. Our team is still settling on its own calls, so these may change.

**Ready (camera 1).** The director is about to take your camera. Hold your shot steady.

**Take (1).** Your camera goes on the stream now.

**Live, hot or on.** Your camera is on the stream. Only slow, small moves.

**Clear.** You're not on the stream anymore. Make big changes now.

**Push in / pull out.** Zoom in / zoom out.

**Tighten up / go wider (loosen up).** Make the shot one size tighter or wider.

**Pan.** Turn the camera left or right on the tripod.

**Tilt.** Point the camera up or down on the tripod.

**Hold.** Keep the shot where it is.

**Settle.** Finish the move and let the shot come to rest.

## Streaming

**Encoder.** The software or hardware that takes audio and video and turns it into a stream the platform can broadcast.

**Bitrate.** The data rate of the stream. Higher means better quality and more bandwidth needed.

**Dropped frames.** When the encoder can't keep up and skips video frames. Usually a bandwidth or CPU issue.

**Stream key.** The secret string that tells the platform "this stream is from us." Treat it like a password.
