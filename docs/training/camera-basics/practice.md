# Practice: dial in the shot

This is a practice camera. It shows what each setting does to the picture, so you can learn to spot a wrong setting and fix it before the director has to ask.

The picture is a photo from the main camera on a real church stage, about 7 m from the speaker, with a big LED wall across the top of the shot. The standard settings are f/2.8, 180°, ISO 400, 4000 K and tint 0, and the room light is 4000 K. The controls run in the same order as [Dialing in your camera](dial-in.md): shutter, white balance, tint, aperture, ISO, then focus.

## How to use it

- **Free play.** Move one control at a time and watch what changes. Try f/2 and f/16, ISO 6400, a white balance of 2500 K and 8000 K, or a 90° shutter and watch the LED wall.
- **Challenges.** Each one starts with something wrong, like a real Sunday. Read what the director noticed, fix it, then press **Director ready?** to check your work.
- **Camera tools.** Turn on zebras, false color, focus peaking or the histogram to measure the picture instead of trusting your eyes.

!!! info "Why the background stays fairly sharp"
    The practice camera uses real lens math for this camera position. With the speaker this far away, even the widest aperture only softens the background a little. That's normal for church cameras at the back of the room, so focus is about the speaker, not about blurring the wall.
