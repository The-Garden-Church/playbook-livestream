# Practice: dial in the shot

This is a practice camera. It shows what each setting does to the picture, so you can learn to spot a wrong setting and fix it before the director has to ask.

The pictures are photos from a real church stage, taken from two camera positions. Pick one above the picture:

- **Main camera (wide).** The center of the room, about 7 m from the speaker. A big LED wall runs across the top of the shot, so this is the one to practice shutter banding on. Standard settings: f/2.8, 180°, ISO 400, 4000 K, tint 0.
- **Side camera (close).** Farther back and zoomed in tight, about 14 m from the speaker. The speaker's hands are moving, so this is the one to practice motion blur on. Standard settings: f/4, 180°, ISO 400, 4000 K, tint 0.

The room light is 4000 K for both. The controls run in the same order as [Dialing in your camera](dial-in.md): shutter, white balance, tint, aperture, ISO, then focus.

## How to use it

- **Free play.** Move one control at a time and watch what changes. Try f/2 and f/16, ISO 6400, or a white balance of 2500 K and 8000 K. On the side camera, compare a 45° and a 360° shutter and watch the hands.
- **Challenges.** Each one starts with something wrong, like a real Sunday, and picks the camera it happens on. Read what the director noticed, fix it, then press **Director ready?** to check your work.
- **Camera tools.** Turn on zebras, false color, focus peaking or the histogram to measure the picture instead of trusting your eyes.

!!! info "Why the background stays fairly sharp"
    The practice camera uses real lens math for these two positions. With the speaker this far away, even the widest aperture only softens the background a little. That's normal for church cameras at the back of the room, so focus is about the speaker, not about blurring the wall.
