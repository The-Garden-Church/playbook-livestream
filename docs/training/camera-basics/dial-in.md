# Dialing in your camera

Now you know what each control is. This page puts them in order. Run through it during setup, before the service starts, so your shot is right before anyone has to ask.

## The order

Set things in this order. The first two get locked and stay that way. The rest you set for the room and keep an eye on.

1. **Frame rate and shutter.** 30 fps and 180°. Check them, don't change them. See [Shutter](shutter.md).
2. **White balance and tint.** Set them to the team's values for the room, the same on every camera. See [White balance and tint](white-balance.md).
3. **Aperture.** Start at the team's standard setting. Open it up if the shot is too dark. See [Aperture](aperture.md).
4. **ISO or gain.** If the shot is still too dark with the aperture open, raise ISO a stop at a time. See [ISO and gain](iso.md).
5. **Frame and focus.** Zoom to your shot, then check focus with focus assist. See [Zoom and focus](focus-and-zoom.md).

!!! info "Why this order"
    Shutter and white balance are locked so motion, flicker and color match across every camera. Aperture comes before ISO because opening the aperture adds light, while raising ISO only amplifies what's there and adds noise.

## Reading your exposure

"Looks fine to me" isn't a reliable test. Camera screens are small and often brighter or darker than the stream. Your camera has tools that measure brightness for you.

**Zebras.** Stripes appear over any part of the picture that is too bright and losing detail. If zebras show up on a face or a white shirt, the picture is too bright. Close the aperture a little or lower ISO.

**False color.** The camera paints the picture in colors based on how bright each part is. Each color means a brightness level, and one color usually marks good skin brightness. Your camera's manual has the color chart.

**Histogram.** A small graph of all the brightness in the picture, dark on the left and bright on the right. A pile pressed against the right edge means highlights are blowing out. A pile pressed against the left means the shadows are crushed to black.

## When something looks wrong

| What you see | What to try first | What not to do |
|---|---|---|
| Picture too dark | Open the aperture a stop, then raise ISO | Don't slow the shutter |
| Picture too bright | Close the aperture a stop, or lower ISO | Don't speed up the shutter |
| Grainy, noisy picture | Lower ISO and open the aperture instead | |
| Faces look orange | Lower the white balance number a little | Don't switch to auto white balance |
| Faces look blue | Raise the white balance number a little | |
| Faces look green or pink | Adjust tint | |
| Bands rolling across the LED wall | Tell the Team Lead | Don't change the shutter yourself during service |
| Shot looks soft | Zoom in, refocus, zoom back out | |
| Your camera doesn't match the others | Check that white balance, tint and ISO match the team's values | |

## Owning your camera

Owning your camera means you notice problems before the director does. A few habits get you there.

- **Look at your shot like a viewer would.** Every few minutes, ask yourself whether the shot is sharp, bright enough, and framed well.
- **Fix small things right away.** A little focus drift or a slightly dark shot is easy to fix early and obvious later.
- **Make big fixes off air.** If you need a big change, wait until your camera isn't live. Watch your tally light, or listen for the director.
- **Tell the director what you changed.** A quick "Camera 2, I opened up a stop" keeps everyone on the same page.
- **Put it back at teardown.** Return everything to the team's standard settings so the next operator starts clean.

??? note "Our standard settings"
    The Garden Church's current baseline values are on the [Hardware](../../hardware.md#standard-camera-settings) page. If you're from another church, write your own down in one place and have every operator start from them.
