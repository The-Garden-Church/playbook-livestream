# Zoom and focus

This is the part of the job you'll do all service long. On most of our cameras you adjust the lens by hand, spinning two rings on the barrel. Each ring slides glass around inside the lens.

## The zoom ring

The wider ring is usually the **zoom** ring. Turning it slides groups of glass apart or together, which changes the lens's **focal length**, measured in millimeters.

- A **short** focal length (like 35mm) sees a wide view of the room.
- A **long** focal length (like 100mm) sees a narrow slice, so the subject looks bigger and closer.

## The focus ring

The narrower ring is usually the **focus** ring. Turning it nudges one small group of glass a short distance. That decides which **distance** from the camera is sharp. Anything closer or farther away gets softer.

## Zooming can knock your focus out

On many lenses, especially photo zooms, focus shifts a little when you zoom. You framed the shot, it was sharp, you zoomed in, and now it's soft. That's normal. **Check focus again every time you zoom.**

A good habit is to zoom all the way in on the subject's face, focus there where it's easiest to see, and then zoom back out to your shot. Then glance at focus once more.

## Focus assist

Your camera can highlight what's in focus right on its screen. This is often called **focus peaking** or **focus assist**. Sharp edges light up in a color (often green). Turn the focus ring until the edges you care about, like the eyes, light up the most.

## Smooth moves

When the shot is live, move the rings slowly and smoothly. A quick jerk on the zoom ring is very visible on the stream. If you need to make a big change, wait until the director takes a different camera, then fix it.

??? note "Lenses that focus by wire"
    Many modern lenses have no direct mechanical link between the focus ring and the glass. The ring tells a motor how far to move. These lenses may not keep their focus when the camera is turned off and on, so always check focus during setup.

??? note "On Blackmagic cameras"
    Function buttons can be set to turn focus assist on and off. Studio cameras can also use handle-mounted zoom and focus controls (Blackmagic Zoom Demand and Focus Demand) with compatible lenses, so you don't touch the rings at all. The idea is the same: one control slides the zoom glass, the other slides the focus glass.

## What to remember

- The zoom ring changes how much of the room you see.
- The focus ring changes what distance is sharp.
- Check focus every time you zoom.
- Use focus assist during setup, and move smoothly when you're live.
