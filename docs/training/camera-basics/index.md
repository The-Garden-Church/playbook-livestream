# Camera basics

This is where every new camera operator starts. You don't need to know anything about cameras yet.

By the end of these lessons you'll know what each control on a camera physically does, why we set it the way we do, and how to dial in your own camera before the director has to ask. That's what we mean by **owning your camera**. The director can always give you direction, but the goal is that your shot already looks right when they look at it.

These lessons stay away from any one brand of camera, so they work for any church team. Tips for the Blackmagic cameras we use are tucked into notes you can open when you need them.

## How a camera makes a picture

Every camera does the same job. Light from the room comes in through the **lens**, passes through an adjustable hole called the **aperture**, and lands on the **sensor** for a set amount of time controlled by the **shutter**. The sensor turns that light into a signal, and **ISO** (or **gain**) turns that signal up or down. **White balance** then decides what color counts as white.

The brightness of the final picture is called **exposure**. Three of those controls change exposure, and each one does it in a different physical way.

| Control | What it physically is | What it changes besides brightness | How often you touch it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aperture | A hole inside the lens, made of overlapping blades | How much of the scene is sharp (depth of field) | During setup, sometimes during service |
| Shutter | How long the sensor collects light for each frame | How motion looks, and flicker from lights and LED walls | Almost never. We lock it. |
| ISO or gain | An amplifier that turns up the sensor's signal | Noise (grain) | During setup, as the last brightness fix |
| White balance | A setting that tells the camera what white looks like | The color of the whole picture | During setup. We lock it. |
| Zoom and focus | Groups of glass inside the lens that slide on rails | How much of the room you see, and what's sharp | Constantly. This is your main job. |

## The lessons

Work through these in order. Each one has a figure you can play with. Drag the controls and watch what moves.

1. [Aperture](aperture.md). The hole that lets light in.
2. [Shutter](shutter.md). Why the camera says 180°, and why we never change it.
3. [ISO and gain](iso.md). The volume knob for light.
