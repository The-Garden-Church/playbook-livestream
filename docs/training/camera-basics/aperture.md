# Aperture

The aperture is a hole inside the lens. A ring of thin metal blades overlaps to make that hole bigger or smaller. A bigger hole lets more light reach the sensor. A smaller hole lets less through.

## f-numbers run backwards

Aperture is measured in **f-numbers**, written like f/2.8 or f/8. The confusing part is that a **smaller number means a bigger hole**. f/2 is wide open and lets in lots of light. f/16 is a pinhole and lets in very little.

It helps to read f/4 as a fraction, "one fourth." One fourth is bigger than one sixteenth, so f/4 is a bigger opening than f/16.

## Stops

The standard f-numbers are 2, 2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11 and 16. Each step along that list is called a **stop**. Every stop lets in half as much light as the one before it. Going from f/2.8 to f/4 cuts the light in half. Going back to f/2.8 doubles it.

You'll hear "open up a stop" (smaller f-number, brighter) and "stop down" (bigger f-number, darker). Stops are the common language for brightness. A stop of aperture, a stop of shutter and a stop of ISO all change brightness by the same amount.

## It also changes what's sharp

The size of the hole changes more than brightness. A wide-open aperture (small f-number) gives a **shallow depth of field**. Only a thin slice of distance is sharp, and the background goes soft. A small aperture (big f-number) keeps much more of the room in focus.

For a pastor on stage, a moderate aperture like f/4 is a good middle ground. It's bright enough for most rooms and forgiving enough that focus doesn't slip every time someone steps forward.

## Where the control is

Some lenses have an aperture ring on the barrel. Many modern lenses, including most Micro Four Thirds zooms, have no ring at all. You set the aperture from the camera's buttons or touchscreen, and a motor inside the lens moves the blades.

??? note "On Blackmagic cameras"
    Tap the f-number at the top of the touchscreen to change it, or use the iris button to set it automatically once and then leave it. Some zoom lenses can't open as wide when zoomed in. If your f-number jumps when you zoom, that's the lens, not a mistake.

## What to remember

- The aperture is a hole made of blades inside the lens.
- Smaller f-number means bigger hole, more light, softer background.
- Each stop is half or double the light.
